Learning to distinguish forest cover types.

This project addresses forest cover type classification. It is included as computational preparation alongside my research interests in human-centered AI and NLP.

I used a stratified 20,000-record Covertype sample with 54 features and seven classes. The dataset citation, version, and sampling seed will be added with the report.

Attribution matters.

I benchmarked classifiers, fitted training-only preprocessing, and evaluated performance. I compared SGD, Adam-family optimizers, and randomized final-layer optimization under controlled compute budgets, examining learning rate, momentum, and regularization. I also evaluated K-Means and Gaussian Mixture Models alongside PCA, ICA, and randomized projections, measuring cluster quality, label alignment, and downstream neural-network performance. Collaborator attribution will be added when confirmed.

Evidence before numbers.

My CV reports an increase in Adam test Macro-F1 from 0.6193 to 0.6710 with label smoothing, an absolute increase of 0.0517. These are reported results; they have not been independently reproduced here.

The comparison supports the importance of regularization in this experiment. It does not establish statistical significance or superiority across datasets. Exact model configurations and the full classifier comparison are needed to interpret the result in context.

The reported experiment uses a sample of the dataset and one documented split. Generalization beyond that setup, sensitivity to sampling and random seeds, and class-level errors require the full evaluation record. A confusion matrix and uncertainty analysis would help make these limitations inspectable.
